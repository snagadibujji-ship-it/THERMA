# 04 — Thermoacoustic Foundations

## Working principle
A thermoacoustic system uses oscillating pressure and velocity fields in a working gas. Gas parcels move back and forth around equilibrium positions. The gas is not required to circulate continuously through the entire machine.

## Compression and expansion
During an acoustic cycle, compression raises local pressure and temperature while expansion lowers them. The timing of those changes relative to gas motion and solid heat exchange determines whether net thermal pumping or acoustic power production occurs.

## Gas-solid thermal exchange
A stack or regenerator provides solid surface area for periodic gas-solid heat transfer. The thermal boundary layer near solid surfaces is therefore fundamental to the geometry.

## Phase relationship
Useful thermoacoustic power transfer depends on the phase relation between pressure and particle velocity. An acoustic pressure amplitude without useful phase behavior is not sufficient to define a productive system.

## Resonance and impedance
The acoustic path presents impedance. Tube lengths, areas, gas volumes, compliance and inertance shape the standing or traveling acoustic field. Exact geometry must be solved rather than estimated by visual proportions.

## Boundary layers
Viscous and thermal boundary layers define the distance over which oscillatory effects interact strongly with the wall. Their scale influences regenerator pore size and surface spacing.

## Engine direction
When an appropriate thermal gradient is established, thermoacoustic processes can amplify an acoustic field and produce acoustic work.

## Refrigerator direction
When acoustic work is supplied in the appropriate phase and geometry, the thermoacoustic process can pump heat from a colder region toward a warmer region.

## Heat-pump direction
The same broad heat-pumping physics can provide useful high-temperature heat when acoustic work is supplied and the thermal routing is configured accordingly.

## THERMA interpretation
THERMA does not invent the underlying physics. The project hypothesis concerns integration: controlled source interfaces, fixed modular acoustic cores, adaptive routing, reversible operation, serviceability, and waste-heat integration.

## Important distinction
The acoustic wave is not a literal pipe carrying heat from one end to the other. Net thermal transport emerges from the coupled pressure, velocity, gas temperature, solid temperature, boundary layers, phase, and geometry.

## Next-level validation
A THERMA model should ultimately be checked against a published experimental geometry before being trusted for THERMA-specific performance prediction. This follows the evidence discipline and the prototype strategy already defined for the project.
